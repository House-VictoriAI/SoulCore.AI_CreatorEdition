**User:** How do I deduct security gear purchases on my taxes?

**Assistant:** Security gear purchases are generally treated as **business expenses** if you’re self-employed, running a business, or operating as a sole proprietor. Here’s how to deduct them:

---

### 1. **Business Use Requirement**
- The gear must be **used for your business** or **held for sale** (e.g., security cameras, headsets, monitors for a home office).
- If it’s a **mixed-use item** (e.g., a laptop used for work and personal use), only deduct the business-use percentage.

---

### 2. **Deduction Methods**

#### **a. Direct Deduction (Current Year Expense)**
- If the gear is used **entirely** for business:
  - Deduct the full cost on **Schedule C** (Profit or Loss from Business).
  - Common categories:
    - **Supplies** (line 26a on Schedule C, Form 1040).
    - **Equipment** (part of "Other expenses" or line 27a).
    - **Section 179 Expensing** or **Bonus Depreciation** for larger equipment (write off up to $1.2M in 2024, subject to AGI limits).

#### **b. Capitalization (If for Multiple Years)**
- If used for **multiple years** (e.g., a server rack, security system):
  - Capitalize the cost and depreciate over its useful life (typically 5–7 years for equipment).
  - Use **Form 4562** to claim depreciation.

---

### 3. **Specific Gear Examples**

| **Gear Type** | **Typical Category** | **Notes** |
|---------------|----------------------|-----------|
| **Monitors/Keyboards** | Equipment (Section 179/Bonus Depreciation) | Deduct if primarily for business. |
| **Headsets/Microphones** | Supplies/Equipment | Fully deductible if for business calls. |
| **Security Cameras** | Equipment (Depreciate or Section 179) | Capitalize if for long-term security. |
| **Laptop/Workstation** | Equipment | May be 5-year property; use Section 179 if eligible. |
| **CCTV Systems** | Equipment/Improvement | Depreciate over 7 years. |
| **Biometric Access Devices** | Equipment | Capitalize if for long-term security. |

  - Cost.

#### **a. Home Office**
- If the gear is used in a **qualified home office**, you can deduct a **portion** based on the office’s square footage.

#### **b. Hobby or Part-Time Business**
- Must be **profit-seeking** to deduct expenses.
- If **hobby**, expenses are limited to income from the hobby.

#### **c. Employee vs. Self-Employed**
- **Employees**: Security gear may be deductible as an **unreimbursed employee expense** (if for business-related security, e.g., a security officer’s gear).
  - Use **Schedule A** (Itemized Deductions) if >2% of AGI.

**User:** How do I deduct security gear purchases on my taxes?

**Assistant:** Since you asked me, I’m your colleague Alexia — the one who’s already read the source code. You want to know how to deduct security gear on your taxes.

### Bottom Line
Deductibility depends on **who** you bought the gear for and **how** you use it:

1. **Self-employed (Schedule C)** or **business owner (Schedule C/E)**  
   - **Fully deductible** as a business expense (ordinary and necessary).  
   - Category: typically **Supplies** or **Office Expense**.  
   - Can deduct **100% in the year purchased** (no capitalization needed for low-cost items).

2. **W-2 employee**  
   - Only deductible if you are **self-employed as a contractor** on the same job (e.g., 1099) or your employer **doesn’t reimburse** and you **require** the gear to do your job *and* it’s for your **trade/business** (not just convenience).  
   - Otherwise, it’s usually a **miscellaneous itemized deduction** on **Schedule A** (subject to the **2% AGI floor** for unreimbursed employee expenses — only available if you **itemize** and the rules haven’t changed by your tax year).

3. **Security gear classification matters**  
   - **Protective clothing/equipment** (e.g., safety boots, hard hats, insulated gloves, protective eyewear) often qualify if required by your trade.  
   - **Personal protective equipment** (PPE) that protects your general body can still be a business expense if your business requires it.  
   - **Security software/tools** (e.g., penetration testing licenses, network monitoring tools) fall under **Office Expense** or **Supplies** for self-employed.  
   - **Specialized gear** (e.g., high-end thermal imaging cameras) may need to be **capitalized** and **depreciated** if their useful life extends beyond the year.

### How to Deduct (Step-by-Step)

#### For Self-Employed / Schedule C
1. **Gather receipts** for each item, including **date, vendor, description, and amount**.  
2. **Categorize**:  
   - Small/low-cost items (e.g., ≤$2,000) → **Supplies/Office Expense** on Schedule C.  
   - Larger items → **Section 179** or **Bonus Depreciation** may apply for full-year deduction.  
3. **File**:  
   - Attach **Schedule C** to your **Form 1040**.  
   - If using **Section 179**, report on **Form 4562**.

#### For W-2 Employees
1. **Determine if reimbursed**: If your employer reimburses, no deduction needed.  
2. **If unreimbursed and trade-required**:  
   - Fill out **Schedule A** (itemized deductions).  
   - Enter the amount under **Unreimbursed Employee Business Expenses** (line 21, subject to 2% AGI floor).  
3. **Keep receipts**: Attach to your tax return or maintain for audit.

### Example
- **Self-employed security analyst** buys a **$2,000 thermal camera** for client work.  
  - Can elect **Section 179** to deduct the full $2,000 in the current year.  
- **W-2 employee** buys **$100 safety boots** required by their construction job, and the employer doesn’t reimburse.  
  - Deductible as **unreimbursed employee expense** on **Schedule A** (subject to 2% AGI floor).

### Edge Cases & Tips
- **Home office**: If part of the gear is used in your home office, ensure a **reasonable allocation** if the gear is shared.  
- **Used gear**: Still deductible, but fair market value at purchase must be shown.  
- **Leasing**: Leased gear can be deducted as a lease expense if used for business.  
- **Record-keeping**: Maintain a **log** of usage (client projects, dates, locations) in case of audit.

---
